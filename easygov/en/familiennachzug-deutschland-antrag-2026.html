Family Reunification Germany: Bringing Your Loved Ones Home (2026)

Category: Residency & Citizenship | Updated: June 2026

"The hardest part of moving to Germany isn't the cold weather or the complex taxes—it's the empty chair at the dinner table."

If you're reading this, you've likely already taken the brave step of building a life in Germany. Now, you’re ready for the most important phase: bringing your family to join you. Family reunification (Familiennachzug) is more than just a visa application; it’s a reunion of hearts. However, the German authorities (BAMF and local Consulates) view this as a purely legal alignment of your living space and financial capacity with your family's needs.

Why Germany Places Such Strict Requirements

You might wonder why they demand proof of apartment size or monthly income. From a legal standpoint, Germany wants to ensure that once your family arrives, they have a sustainable, dignified life. They want to avoid a scenario where a family arrives without the basic infrastructure—housing, health insurance, and financial security—to integrate properly. Understanding this logic helps you prepare your application not as a supplicant, but as a provider proving he/she is ready.

The Master Checklist: What You Actually Need

To keep your family together, your documentation must be flawless. Missing even one apostille can cause months of delay.

  • The "Living Space" Verification: A lease agreement explicitly stating the square footage is non-negotiable.
  • Proof of Sustenance: Your last 6 months of bank statements and employment contracts (Work-life balance matters).
  • Marriage/Birth Certificates: Must be originals with apostilles (do not try to submit copies).

Avoiding the "Rejection Spiral"

Most applications fail not because the family doesn't belong together, but because the applicant underestimated the "Living Space" (Wohnraum) rule. If your apartment is too small, no amount of money will get that application approved. Pro Tip: If your current apartment is too small, look for a new place *before* applying. It’s an investment in your family’s future.
